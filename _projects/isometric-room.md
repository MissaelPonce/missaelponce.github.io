Personal project to sharpen my 3D modelling, composition, and lighting workflow in Blender. I modelled most of the scene from scratch — desk, shelves, decor, and small props — and used a couple of free add-on assets (a gamer chair and some plants) to speed up iteration.

## What I Focused On

**Modelling** — building clean, efficient geometry for everyday interior objects with topology I could iterate on quickly.

**Isometric composition** — framing the scene so it reads clearly from a fixed camera with no perspective distortion. Isometric views are unforgiving: every element needs to earn its place in the frame, because there's nowhere to hide behind depth.

**Lighting** — experimenting with setups to create mood and depth within a stylised aesthetic without blowing out the scene or flattening it.

The scene was kept intentionally efficient so I could test different renders without long wait times — a constraint that pushed me to develop better habits around polygon count and scene complexity early on.

## Outcome

The project refined my modelling workflow and lighting approach for stylised interiors, and gave me a solid baseline for the optimised scene management that becomes critical in longer-term environment art work.
